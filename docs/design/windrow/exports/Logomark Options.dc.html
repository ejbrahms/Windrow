Windrow logomark — 5 fresh trail/row directions

A windrow is the raked row of material left behind a pass — hay, leaves, snow — not moving air. The shipped mark and both prior "curl" directions (1b, 1c in Logomark Concepts.dc.html) read as breeze/puff icons because they used arcs curling back on themselves. None of the 5 below use a closed loop or curl — every shape is straight-edged and either shortens, thins, or lowers left→right to say "the row runs out here," which is the one visual idea a windrow actually has that wind doesn't.

Each option shown three ways: an 76px detail view, the real 24px nav-chip size next to the wordmark, and the real 16px favicon-tab size — the sizes this mark actually ships at. No winner is picked here; see HANDOFF.md addendum 4 for the SVG markup of all 5.

Three tapered diagonal bars, each shorter than the last, all sloping the same direction. Straight taper (not a curved wedge like round-1's 1a) means the silhouette stays crisp at 16px. The three different lengths — not just three parallel copies — are what sells "this row ran out sooner than that one," a windrow cross-section, not moving air.

Three flat horizontal bars, left-aligned, each shorter than the one above — the most literal "rows of decreasing length" reading of the five, and the plainest at 16px since it's just three solid rectangles with no taper geometry to lose. Single-hue, no stroke — the strict-minimal option, closest to the retracted round-2 monochrome pass but redrawn as flat rows instead of diagonal lines.

A ground baseline with four raked mounds sitting on it, each shorter than the last — a literal cross-section of a windrow (heaped hay/leaves along a line), not an abstraction of one. Grounded on a baseline instead of floating strokes, which is what makes it unambiguously "material on the ground," never air. Single hue keeps it calm at 16px where four separate peaks could otherwise compete for attention.

Four short marks along one falling diagonal, each smaller and lower than the last — a single row of debris fading into the distance, like footprints or scattered material trailing off after one pass. Isolated marks with real gaps between them (unlike 2a/2b's continuous bars) reads as "scattered leftovers," reinforcing material, not airflow. Shrinking size plus dropping position double down on the "fading out" cue, so it still reads at 16px even though the smallest mark is tiny.

One diagonal line — the pass itself — crossed by three short tine-marks that shrink toward the top end. The long line reads as the row/furrow directly (a windrow is fundamentally a line across a field); the ticks read as the rake's texture on it, not as curls. No arc anywhere in this one at all, the strongest "definitely not wind" option, though it's the most linework-heavy of the five and worth checking against an actual 16px render for tick legibility.
